What makes this house different

The machine keeps the book.

Traditional cockpits run on the kristo, taking bets by shout and gesture and settling on memory. Infanta does not. Every wager here goes through the betting machine and comes back as a printed ticket, so what you staked and what you are owed is never a matter of anyone's word.

01

Place it at the machine

Choose your side, meron or wala, and the amount. No shouting across the gallery, no hand signals to misread.

02

Take your ticket

The machine prints your wager: fight number, side, stake. That ticket is your proof, and it does not depend on anyone remembering you.

03

Collect on the result

The result is recorded against the fight. Present the ticket and collect. The book balances because it was never kept by hand.

Know before you go

How the fighting is run

DERBY

Teams, not single birds

Entries are made as a team, usually three, five or seven cocks. The entry taking the most wins across the card takes the derby. It tests a whole breeding programme, not one lucky bird.

TRADITIONAL

Matched on the day

Single fights, paired on the day once the birds are weighed and matched by the arena. The backbone of any regular fight day.

ON THE DAY

What to expect

  • Arrive early for a seat with a clear line to the pit.
  • Bets are registered at the machine, not by hand signal.
  • Keep your ticket. It is your claim on the result.
  • Handlers and the referee are not to be interfered with.

The drive

Over the Sierra Madre,
down to the sea.

Infanta sits on the Pacific side of Luzon, and the way in is the Marikina–Infanta Highway, the road everyone calls Marilaque. A hundred and seventeen kilometres from Quezon City, climbing through the Sierra Madre and dropping to the coast. People make the drive for its own sake. A derby at the end of it is a good enough reason to go.

Km 0
Quezon City
Marilaque begins at Katipunan and heads east through Marikina.
The climb
Antipolo & Tanay
Up into the Sierra Madre through Rizal. The stretch riders come for.
The crossing
Sta. Maria, Laguna
Forest and ridgeline, then the long descent toward the coast.
Km 117.5
Infanta, Quezon
The road ends at the Pacific. The arena is on the way into town.
BY CAR

Roughly four hours

From Metro Manila by way of Marilaque, traffic and weather depending. Mountain road: take the curves seriously, especially in the wet.

BY BUS & VAN

Daily services

Vans and buses run from Metro Manila and from Lucena toward Infanta and Real through the day.

ALTERNATIVE

Via Famy

The Famy–Real–Infanta Road comes in along the coast, a gentler run for anyone avoiding the mountain.

{# Infanta is a Pacific coastal town. Dinahican is its own beach and fishport, and Real next door has some of the closest surf to Manila. Worth saying plainly: it turns a fight day into a weekend. #}
Make a weekend of it

The Pacific is
ten minutes away.

Infanta faces open ocean. Dinahican is the town’s own beach and fishport, where the boats come in and the catch is sold off the pier. Along the coast at Real are some of the nearest surf breaks to Manila. Fight in the afternoon, eat fish by the water, sleep to the sound of the sea.

DINAHICAN

Beach & fishport

Infanta’s own shoreline and working fishport. Fish straight off the boats, and resorts along the sand.

REAL

Surf next door

The neighbouring town is a surf destination in its own right, with breaks suited to beginners and boards to rent.

SIERRA MADRE

Falls & rivers

The mountains behind the town hold waterfalls and cold rivers within a short drive of the arena.

The house

Built so every seat
sees the pit.

Infanta Sports Arena opened in 2026 on the road into town, red against the rice fields with the range behind it. A clear-span roof with no columns breaking the gallery, and a pit set so the whole room watches the same fight at the same moment.
